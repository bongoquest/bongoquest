If you are looking for an accessible, stylish, and fun urban motorcycle under the ₹1.7 lakh (ex-showroom) price mark, two models likely stand out on your radar: the Royal Enfield Hunter 350 and the TVS Ronin 225.

While both motorcycles target city riders looking for retro-inspired aesthetics, they approach the daily commute with completely different personalities. Here is a breakdown of how they compare across design, performance, features, and everyday usability to help you pick the right ride.  

1. Design & Looks: Pure Roadster vs. Tech Scrambler

Royal Enfield Hunter 350: The Hunter embraces a classic neo-retro roadster stance. With its teardrop fuel tank, round headlight, clean lines, and compact proportions, it offers the signature Royal Enfield character in a much tighter, sportier body.

TVS Ronin 225: The Ronin is a mashup of styles—part scrambler, part cruiser, and part modern retro. It sports a muscular tank, a signature T-shaped LED DRL, dynamic paneling, and an asymmetrical instrument cluster.  

Verdict: If you prefer timeless, sleek roadster aesthetics, the Hunter 350 takes the crown. If you like modern, futuristic retro designs that turn heads with techy details, go for the Ronin.

2. Engine & Performance: Torque vs. Tractability

Despite the difference in displacement (349cc vs. 225.9cc), both bikes produce almost identical peak power outputs, but they deliver that power very differently:

Hunter 350: The larger engine delivers a thick wave of low-end torque (27\text{ Nm}). You get that easy, low-RPM pull without needing constant gear shifts, along with the deep, reassuring Royal Enfield exhaust rumble.  

Ronin 225: Thanks to being 22 kg lighter (159\text{ kg} vs. 181\text{ kg}), the Ronin feels peppy and nimble. Its engine revs more freely, and the light clutch makes navigating heavy bumper-to-bumper traffic effortless.  

3. Handling & Ride Comfort

City Agility: The Ronin feels lighter on its feet. Parking, low-speed u-turns, and filtering through traffic are easier on the TVS.  

Suspension & Bad Roads: The Ronin comes fitted with 41mm Upside Down (USD) front forks and a rear monoshock, which cushion potholes and rough roads exceptionally well. The Hunter uses traditional telescopic forks and twin shock absorbers, giving it a firmer, sportier ride.  

Ground Clearance: The Ronin offers 181 mm of ground clearance compared to the Hunter’s 160 mm, making the Ronin better equipped for tall speed breakers and broken pavement.  

4. Tech & Equipment

TVS is known for loading its motorcycles with features, and the Ronin is no exception:

TVS Ronin 225: Offers full LED lighting, a fully digital off-set instrument cluster, Bluetooth connectivity with turn-by-turn navigation, voice assist, call/SMS alerts, riding modes (Rain/Urban ABS modes), and a slipper clutch.  

Royal Enfield Hunter 350: Keeps things functional and retro-modern with a digital-analog speedometer. Tripper turn-by-turn navigation is available as an official accessory or on higher trims.  

5. Mileage & Ownership Cost

Fuel Economy: The TVS Ronin averages around 40–42 km/l, whereas the Hunter 350 delivers roughly 35–37 km/l under mixed city and highway conditions.

Maintenance & Warranty: TVS offers a longer standard warranty and lower fuel expenditure, while Royal Enfield boasts an extensive service network and high resale value.  

The Final Verdict: Which One Should You Buy?

Pick the Royal Enfield Hunter 350 if:

You love the iconic Royal Enfield brand identity, sound, and retro design.

You prefer effortless low-end torque for relaxed city cruising.  

You want a sportier seating posture with slightly rear-set footpegs for spirited cornering.  

Pick the TVS Ronin 225 if:

You want a lightweight, feature-packed motorcycle with smart connectivity.  

Comfort over rough roads and light clutch action are top priorities for your daily commute.  

You prefer better fuel efficiency and higher ground clearance.
